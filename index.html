How it works

Most apps hand you a twelve-week programme on day one and hope you fit it. Gym Logger writes one workout at a time, so the plan is always built on what you did last, not what someone guessed you'd do.

Tell it about you

Your goal, your experience, the equipment you have, the days you can train, anything you're working around.

Get your first week

A roadmap of training stages and a first week of sessions built for your kit and your schedule, in about a minute.

Train, and the next one's written

Finish a workout and the next is generated from the weights and reps you logged. Progress is earned from the log, never scheduled.

Built for the gym floor

Everything you need mid-set, and nothing you have to think about.

Rest timer that follows you

Starts when you log a set, counts down on the Lock Screen and Dynamic Island, and lets you skip or finish the next set without unlocking.

Form guidance on every movement

Each exercise carries a cue and an animated demonstration, so an unfamiliar name is never a reason to skip it.

Last time, right there

Every set shows what you did the previous session, so beating it is one keystroke and progress is visible as you make it.

Challenges

Six-week plans toward a number: a set of push-ups, strict pull-ups, a bodyweight bench. Scaled to your level, run alongside your main plan.

A training log you'll look at

A calendar of everything you've done, with sets, volume and time per session and per month.

Your music, uninterrupted

Timer sounds mix with whatever you're playing instead of pausing it.

How the plans are built

The generator follows a fixed set of programming rules, and they're published in the app under every unit. The short version:

  • Finishable. Sessions are sized to the time you said you have. An abandoned workout is worse than an easy one.
  • Weekly volume, not daily. Hard sets per muscle group are budgeted across the week and scaled to your experience.
  • Reps in reserve. Most work stops one to three reps short of failure. Nothing technical is ever taken to failure.
  • Double progression. Add reps within the target first, then add load and start again.
  • Deload on evidence. Two missed sessions in a row cut the volume. There's no scheduled taper because the plan doesn't end.
  • Injuries outrank everything. Report one and the next workout is written around it.
